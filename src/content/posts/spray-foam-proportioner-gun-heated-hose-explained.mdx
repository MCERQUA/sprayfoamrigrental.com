If you've spent any time researching spray foam equipment, you've probably run into three
terms fast: proportioner, gun, and heated hose. They're usually mentioned together because
they work together — a spray foam "rig" isn't one machine, it's a system, and each piece has a
specific job.

## The Proportioner: The Heart of the System

The proportioner is the core unit. Spray foam is made of two chemical components — usually
called the A side and B side — that have to be heated, pressurized, and mixed in a precise 1:1
ratio before they reach the gun. That's the proportioner's whole job.

Get that ratio even slightly wrong, and the foam that comes out the other end can look fine on
the surface but cure improperly underneath — weaker structure, poor adhesion, or cells that
don't set up the way they should. That's why proportioner output rating and calibration matter
so much when evaluating equipment: it's not just "does it spray foam," it's "does it spray foam
on-ratio, consistently, for the size of job you're doing."

## The Spray Gun: Where It All Comes Together

The gun is the application tool — it's what actually mixes the heated, pressurized chemicals
at the tip and lays down the foam. Gun type and maintenance condition affect finish quality
directly: a worn or poorly maintained gun can produce an inconsistent spray pattern even when
the proportioner upstream is dialed in perfectly.

## The Heated Hose: Keeping Temperature Consistent Over Distance

Here's the part people underestimate. The chemicals have to stay at the right temperature not
just at the proportioner, but all the way through the hose run to the gun — sometimes 100+
feet, depending on the rig setup. A heated hose maintains that temperature along the entire
run. If it can't hold temperature consistently, you get the same off-ratio problems as a
miscalibrated proportioner, just introduced further down the line.

When you're comparing rental listings, "spray foam rig" can mean very different equipment
depending on the proportioner's output capacity, the gun's condition, and the hose length and
heating reliability. Two rigs advertised for the same day rate can produce very different
