# Content Brief: What Equipment Is Typically Available to Rent

## Purpose
Neutral equipment-type education — the informational counterpart to every competitor's product
catalog (see gaps-opportunities.md #2). Ranks for equipment-type + rental queries and supports
the Rent vs. Buy and What-to-Check pages internally.

## Target keywords
Primary: spray foam equipment rental, spray foam rental equipment
Secondary: spray foam proportioner rental, spray foam gun rental, heated hose rental, spray foam
generator rental, spray foam air compressor rental, closed cell spray foam machine rental, open
cell spray foam equipment rental

## Search intent
Informational, with commercial undertones (visitors here are actively researching a rental).

## Page sections
1. **Intro** — what a full "rig" actually consists of (most searchers conflate "the rig" with a
   single machine; it's a system).
2. **Proportioner** — the core plural-component metering/heating unit; explain what it does,
   why capacity/output rating matters for job size.
3. **Spray gun** — application tool, why gun type/maintenance affects finish quality.
4. **Heated hose** — why temperature control along the hose run matters for material chemistry
   and finish; typical length ranges.
5. **Generator / power requirements** — electric vs. gas-powered rigs, on-site power needs,
   why this affects where/how a rig can be used.
6. **Material types (open cell vs. closed cell)** — brief, factual explainer (properties, typical
   use cases) — informational only, no claim of selling/renting material.
7. **What's usually bundled vs. rented separately** — set expectations that rental packages vary
   by provider (ties to gaps-opportunities' pricing-inconsistency finding).
8. **CTA** — soft: "Whichever setup you're evaluating, if you'd rather hand the job to a pro,
   get matched with an insured spray foam contractor."

## Internal links
→ Rent vs. Buy, What to Check Before You Rent, relevant blog posts (e.g. proportioner explainer)

## Word count target
1,000-1,400 words

## Notes
Zero brand/model endorsement language (no "the best proportioner is..."); keep to generic,
factual equipment education matching the site's honest-guide tone.
